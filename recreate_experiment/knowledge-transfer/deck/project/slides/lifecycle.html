Compared with what runs: counts and a remainder

Build, downstream. Each stage writes its artefacts and loses something at the handoff. By the time the loss reaches the code it looks like a decision.

Recover, upstream. Start from what runs and work back one stage at a time. At each stage a person adds what the system cannot give (◇).

Validate, downstream again. Check each recovered stage, rebuild from the record alone, and compare the rebuild with what runs. The dashed line is that comparison.

Preserving tacit and tribal knowledge in banking systems

The same five layers as slide 2, now as columns, upstream on the left and downstream on the right. Three passes run over them. The first is the build as it ran, left to right: each stage writes its artefacts and loses something at the handoff, and the losses add up, which is the cascade of slide 7. The second is the reverse engineering of the pilot, right to left: it starts from what runs, the traces and the code, and works back one stage at a time to the rules. At each stage a person adds what the system cannot give, the reason, the assumption, the exception, the name. Those are the diamonds, and they are the sessions of weeks 3 to 6. The third pass is validation, left to right again: each recovered stage is checked with the tools of slide 10, the record is rebuilt from the writing alone, and the rebuild is compared with what runs. The dashed line closes the loop. The comparison gives counts and a remainder, never a percentage, and the rebuild is never used to repair the record. The next slide is the same pilot as a calendar.
