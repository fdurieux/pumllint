Independent evidence and sources

Implicit knowledge of a code base is a measured cost

METR, an independent AI-evaluation non-profit, ran a randomised controlled trial in 2025: 16 experienced maintainers, 246 real issues in their own mature repositories. With AI the developers took 19 percent longer, after forecasting a 24 percent speed-up. The factor METR names first is the developers’ implicit knowledge of their own repositories, which the AI never saw because nothing forced it to be explicit.

The 2026 continuation measured smaller slowdowns with intervals spanning zero and a selection effect, so the headline number is a 2025 artefact. What survives is the finding that unstated context is a real cost and that explicit, checkable context is the countermeasure.
